# RESIDUAL_FAILURES: hand reading of the 15 failed C1 episodes of the pilot

Read-only, no model, no ROS. All 15 valid C1 episodes that were not supported-complete (lfm3 3, gpt-oss 5, bonsai 7) were read request by request from the raw traces (`view.py` prints one trace with line numbers; the automatic taxonomy labels were not used). "L" is the line number in that episode's `trace.jsonl` (path in `RESULTS_LOG.jsonl`; run ids below). Request numbers (R) count `model_end` events. One reader (Claude); a reading, not a measurement. The scorer's own output (exact flag, support-missing list, answer after removing an `{"answer": ...}` wrapper) was used only to confirm what I read, never to choose a cause.

## Summary
- **6 of the 15 are scoring-convention failures, not wrong answers.** gpt-oss 5 of 5 (four wrote `{"answer": {...}}` as plain text instead of calling `final_answer`, and the scorer's last-object rule reads the wrapper; one read a node as `plant32/oasis` without the leading slash and the strict support rule wants the exact full name), bonsai 1 (a correct "not running" answer whose only evidence is a lookup of `alder/pewter` without the slash). In all six the model's content was correct and the needed facts were in a tool result.
- **9 are real model-side failures:** lfm3 3 (two answers shift the chain by one node, one stops after a service lookup and never finds the provider), bonsai 6 (wrong node read from a correct result 1; a different node taken for the asked one 1; guessed an answer after not finding the node 1; short node names in a correct-data answer 1; misread a correct result and ended in prose 1; mistook a parameter task for a topic task and looped 40 times 1).
- **Needed information in a tool result:** yes in 12 of 15 (all six artifacts, lfm3 p3 x2, bonsai amb_2, abs_2 s2, h4, u3); **no** in 3 (lfm3 v2, bonsai v2, bonsai chg_2). **Stopped early:** 1 (lfm3 v2 stopped after 3 requests, skipping the provider lookup); bonsai u3 did not stop early but ended in prose without an answer at R12; the others used their requests. **Wrong node or field read:** bonsai amb_2 (read the wrong node), bonsai abs_2 s2 (took a decoy node for the asked one), bonsai chg_2 (treated `thistle`/`flow` as a topic), bonsai v2 (searched node names instead of reading node_info); lfm3 p3 x2 and bonsai h4 reported the right data in the wrong field or form.

## Notes for reading the numbers
- Rows 4 to 8 and 11 are the 6 artifacts. They make the pilot's C1 supported-complete counts lower than the content-correct counts for gpt-oss (5 of 20 fails, none a content error) and bonsai (1 of 7). The wrapper comes from gpt-oss answering in the shape of the `final_answer` arguments as plain text (in these four episodes it never called the tool), which the last-JSON-object rule does not unwrap. This is a limit of the scoring rules I used, stated in the pilot report; it was not changed after the run.
- None of the nine model-side failures has `ros2_param_set`, a write tool, or a tool exception as the cause; regex exceptions (rows 12, 15) were returned as text and the models continued.
- Two rows (2, 14) involve a service or subscription lookup where the right field was in or one call away from a result; rows 12 and 15 are the only ones where the information never appeared and the model had no productive next call.

## Addendum 2026-11-15 (after the scorer decision, [scorer v2.0](../../decisions/2026-11-15-scorer-v2.md))
The original text above is unchanged. Under scorer v2.0 rows 4 to 8 (the five gpt-oss episodes) become supported-complete. **Row 11 (bonsai abs_2 seed 1) stays unsupported and my reading of it as a scoring artifact was wrong:** `slash_check.json` shows `ros2 node info plant32/anvil` fails for a node that exists, so its failed lookup of `alder/pewter` is not evidence of absence; the answer was right without valid evidence. So the 15 failures become 9 real model-side failures, 1 correct-but-unsupported answer (row 11) and 5 scoring artifacts (rows 4 to 8).
