## Top box
- **Run:** 288 of 288 episodes (18 development tasks x 4 models x new seeds 5-8), laptop, FULL arm of the thinking runner, no episode retried, 0 infrastructure failures, 0 GPU-contended episodes (the laptop's record cannot see other processes, see limits), `merge-check` 0 duplicates / 0 missing. **Supported-complete 243 of 288 (84.4%)** (replication FULL on the same tasks, seeds 3-4: 125 of 144, 86.8%). By model: qwen3.5 70/72, gpt-oss 67/72, lfm3 58/72, bonsai 48/72.
- **Requests: 1,655 against a floor of 832; 828 requests (50% of all requests) are above the floor.** By model, excess as a share of its requests: bonsai 64% (367 excess), lfm3 56% (263), gpt-oss 37% (118), qwen3.5 28% (80).
- **Where the 828 excess requests go (frozen classifier, one primary type each, in precedence order):** batchable serial reads 248 (30%), identical repeats 157 (19%), name guessing 147 (18%), recovery after a guess 71 (9%), format / interface 75 (9%), unexplained longer route 130 (16%). **Hand-check and post-hoc caveat:** 36 of the 325 requests typed `serial_batchable` (11%; 29 of gpt-oss's 93) are only a retry of a call the harness had just rejected, which the frozen rule cannot tell from batching (sections 3, 5).
- **Concentration:** 5 episodes that ran into the 40-request cap (bonsai v2 x3, bonsai nf_single_2, lfm3 nf_exact) hold 187 excess requests (23%) and 864,482 of 5,287,125 input tokens (16%); the 28 episodes with the most excess (top 10%) hold 54.5% of all excess. Task `v2` alone has 166 excess requests (20%).
- **Waste sits in successful episodes as well:** 462 of the 828 excess requests (56%) are in the 243 supported episodes (152 of them have at least one excess request); 366 (44%) are in the 45 unsupported ones.
- **Failures (45 unsupported episodes):** **wrong conclusion with every support fact present 26 (58%)**, no answer 9 (cap 5, backend error 3, rejected last request 1), facts never read 10 (loop 4, format 1, other 5). Of the 26 wrong conclusions about half involve a name written in the wrong form (13 have a `name_form` issue, 6 a wrong entity, 7 a missing or extra answer field, heuristic); 7 episodes (lfm3 2, qwen3.5 1, bonsai 4) report a **non-existent entity as present**.
- **Stability against the development episodes (230, seeds 1-4, same classifier):** the shares of qwen3.5 and gpt-oss move by 0-6 points; lfm3 and bonsai move a lot (bonsai format 21% -> 6%, name guessing 12% -> 25%) because a few looping episodes dominate their counts. Read per-model shares from 72 episodes with that in mind.

## 3. Hand check of the classifier (author's reading of seeded random samples of the measurement traces, after the frozen run: 14 `serial_batchable`, 8 `repeat`, 8 `name_guess`, 8 `format` requests and 3 `unexplained longer route` episodes read in full)
- `repeat`: 8 of 8 sampled requests really repeat an identical successful call (for example lfm3 nf_exact seed 7 requests 23, 26 and 40: `param_get` of the same parameter returning the same value; `research/_artifacts/exam/lossmap/runs_all/block_0003/007-nf_exact-FULL-lfm3_1.7b-s7/trace.jsonl`).
- `name_guess`: 8 of 8 name an entity that is not in the ledger and fail (a short name from the task text such as `bobbin`, an invented node such as `/plant32/cedar`, `/zircon`). By construction **a short name taken from the task text and used as the argument is counted as a guess**; this is the name-binding problem itself, not a classifier error.
- `format`: 8 of 8 are harness-rejected or empty requests (gpt-oss `blacklist: {}` for list tools, bonsai invalid regex patterns such as `*cedar*` -> "multiple repeat at position 2", qwen3.5 a malformed `final_answer`, a request with no call).
- **`serial_batchable`: 10 of 14 sampled requests are what the definition says (an independent read that could have been sent in the previous request); 4 are retries of a call the harness had just rejected (gpt-oss list tools with an invalid `blacklist`, bonsai a regex error).** Post hoc over all 325: 36 retries (lfm3 4, gpt-oss 29, bonsai 3), i.e. 289 are batchable-by-definition. This overlap was not in the frozen rules and is reported, not corrected, in the frozen numbers.
- `unexplained longer route` (130): the 3 episodes read in full show extra productive-looking requests such as `param_list` before `param_get` for each node and sequential per-node reads; it is not one mechanism (post hoc: 107 of 111 successful `param_list` calls are followed later by a `param_get` of the same node: qwen3.5 37 of 37, bonsai 48 of 49, lfm3 21 of 24, gpt-oss 1 of 1). Whether the floor assumes the direct `param_get` is not established.
- Not checked: precision of `name_recovery`, recall of any type, the `wrong_entity` / `name_form` sub-split beyond the 26 listed episodes (one typo-type name was read as `wrong_entity`).

## 4. Reading of the map (interpretation; hypotheses, not findings, no recommendation)
1. **Largest single class is mergeable serial reads (about 250-290 of 828).** It is also the class the detector is least sure of (sections 3, 5), and the models that show it most (qwen3.5 40%, gpt-oss 43%) have the smallest excess. If it is real it is a request count, not an answer-correctness loss: 462 of the excess requests are in episodes that still succeed. Closest prior work with its own negative or neutral evidence: the list-sweep study (whole-episode token use -1.4%, native operations +7.8%), the finish-split and rendering studies; read them before proposing anything.
2. **The volume of lost answers is mostly wrong conclusions with the facts present (26 of 45), and about half of those are names in the wrong form or missing fields.** The applied name rewrite of 2026-11-17 (strict supported 54 -> 62 of 64 on the name-fix tasks, 8 answers rewritten; `name_fix_applied_20261117/REPORT.md`) addresses the name-form part and is not part of FULL here; whether it would have changed these 26 was not tested. The remaining wrong conclusions (wrong entity, absent entity reported present, wrong hop) look different and are small in number (6 + 7).
3. **A few runaway episodes carry a quarter of the excess and of the long-context tokens** (cap endings by bonsai and one lfm3 episode: the same call repeated up to 40 times, e.g. `node_list` or `param_get`). The stall-hint v2 study (closed, failed its offline bar) addressed repeated calls; this study measures their volume only (157 repeat requests, 13 episodes with a loop) and tests nothing.
4. **Interface errors are concentrated in one model:** 40 of gpt-oss's 118 excess requests are format requests, 28 of them the same invalid `blacklist` argument on list tools (followed by a retry, 29 of its `serial_batchable`), and 17 are requests with no tool call. That is a schema-adoption problem of one model, not a general loss (format is 9% of all excess).
5. **The map is model-dependent:** bonsai (4B, Q2) loses mostly to repeats and guessing, lfm3 to guessing and serial reads, gpt-oss to format and serial reads, qwen3.5 has little to lose (excess 80, 2 failures). A mechanism aimed at one class would act on at most one or two of the four models.

## 5. Limits
- **Development tasks only, 18 tasks, one environment family per group**, the same tasks and models were used for design: no generalisation claim, and the map says nothing about held-out tasks or other robots. Counts are not independent samples (same tasks and fixtures across seeds; a task such as v2 contributes 20% of the excess).
- **Per-model counts rest on 72 episodes**; a few looping episodes dominate bonsai and lfm3 (section top box, stability line).
- **The classifier is a detector, frozen before the data and hand-checked afterwards on small samples.** Its weaknesses are stated in `CLASSIFIER_NOTES.md` (item 6) and above (retries counted as batchable; short task names counted as guesses; `serial_batchable` cannot see why a model chose an entity; the floor assumes serial chains and one entity per request). `waste_within_floor` (109) shows the rules and the floor do not agree on every episode.
- **gpt-oss's thinking level cannot be controlled** (its "off" is medium-like); its numbers are descriptive only. **Hardware:** laptop 8 GB card with CPU offload for qwen3.5 and gpt-oss; the lab (16 GB, everything resident) would give separate strata and different outputs.
- **GPU contention:** under WSL `nvidia-smi` lists no processes, so the frozen rule could only use residency; 0 of 288 episodes were flagged and every row has 3 or more samples, but this does not exclude a neighbour process.
- **Run-to-run variation is not measured here** (one draw per seed); the development comparison (section top box) gives a feel for it only.
- Teammate context: `rosa-contexts` was fetched (2026-11-20); the `member/krln` branch holds only the protocol template and `member/levinson` is this repository's mirror, so no teammate report was available and no overlap could be checked beyond that.

5. **`param_list` before every `param_get`** (111 calls, 107 followed by a get of the same node), a habit of qwen3.5 (37 of 37) and bonsai, not of gpt-oss.
6. **Task v2** (find the node that provides a service named by its short name, then the service type) is the hardest: bonsai 0 of 4 and lfm3 0 of 4 supported on it against 4 of 4 for qwen3.5 and 3 of 4 for gpt-oss; 5 of its 9 unsupported episodes name the wrong node (`/plant32/cedar` or `/plant32/cedar_reset`, a name derived from the service name, instead of `/plant32/wharf`), 3 hit the cap and 1 had a backend error.
7. **Run folder structure:** blocks are numbered by queue block, not by model: the example paths above carry the block number; the queue ran 12 blocks (one fixture session per model and environment).
8. **Not seen at all:** write-tool attempts (0), lucky guesses (0), supported-but-not-exact answers (0), episodes that failed to start (0).
9. **Process:** the classifier freeze, the post-hoc script `posthoc.py` and its outputs were written after the measurement data were opened and are labelled so; the frozen tables were not edited. The first mini-exam on the lab and this run both exposed that a rule or sampler can mislabel; the hand check here found the retry overlap only because the sample was read, which suggests reading a sample of each new detector before using its counts.
