# Part A follow-up (CPU only, 2026-11-19): do output tokens include thinking? and the no-answer endings

Requested by the owner after part A. Saved traces of the 160 part-A episodes only; nothing was run. Part A's results and class are unchanged.

## 1. Output tokens include thinking: yes
- `usage.output_tokens` in every trace equals the response's `eval_count` (checked on all 127 escalated requests that returned thinking text).
- Escalated requests with thinking text (n = 127): the number of output tokens tracks the **thinking** characters, not the visible text. Characters per token **if thinking is counted**: lfm3 3.89 (n = 35), qwen3.5 3.78 (7), bonsai 3.93 (70), gpt-oss 4.05 (15); if only the visible text were counted the figures would be 0.43, 1.74, 0.83 and 0.64 (impossible for text). Ordinary requests (thinking off, three models): 3.76 characters per token. Correlation between output tokens and thinking characters over the 127 requests: 0.997.
- **Consequence: the output-token columns of the part A report already include thinking tokens**; no recomputation of the cost table is needed. The report's "thinking tokens (estimate)" row is a subset of the output tokens (output minus the visible tokens), not an addition. gpt-oss FULL requests also contain hidden thinking in their output tokens although the trace does not store it (`reasoning=False`).

**Reading.** Of ESCALATE's six no-answer endings, **three had escalation active** (bonsai `v2` s2: cap during a sustained trigger loop, FULL counterpart supported; lfm3 `v2` s2: thinking exhausted the 4,096-token limit; gpt-oss `h4` s2: empty response, **identical in FULL**, so not attributable) and **three had escalation never active** (gpt-oss `h4` s1, lfm3 `h4` s1, lfm3 `v2` s1): in those, the FULL counterpart's request 1 already differed, i.e. the divergence is run-to-run variation, not the treatment. Two escalation-attributable worse outcomes (bonsai `v2` s2, lfm3 `v2` s2 as a length cut) against two cases where FULL ended with no answer and the ESCALATE counterpart finished (bonsai `e1h_chg_2` s2, lfm3 `e1h_abs_2` s1: in the latter the FULL arm's request 2 raised the backend XML error and the escalated request 2 did not; H: not tested). The 3 -> 6 change is therefore **not shown to be an effect of escalation**; the one mechanism that is visible and costly is **thinking runaway under sustained triggers** (a window that re-arms on every dead end keeps thinking on for 36 of 40 requests in the bonsai case, 12 consecutive requests in the lfm3 case; the length cut at 4,096 tokens is a risk the protocol listed).

## 3. New finding: identical requests do not reproduce between the two arms
Request 1 has an identical prompt and identical settings (seed, temperature, no override) in FULL and ESCALATE, yet its output differs in **22 of 80 cells: gpt-oss 16 of 20, lfm3 4 of 20, qwen3.5 1 of 20, bonsai 1 of 20** (tool-call ids excluded; content, tool names, arguments and token counts compared). gpt-oss, with its hidden thinking and CPU offload, is the least reproducible. Consequences: trajectories diverge **from the first request** for gpt-oss and sometimes lfm3 even where the arms are identical, so paired cells are descriptive only (as the protocol said) and the gpt-oss difference between arms cannot be read at all; for the other models the arms are identical until the first trigger in most cells (cells with no trigger in either arm had the same request count and outcome in 14 of 14 qwen3.5, 7 of 8 bonsai, 6 of 7 lfm3, 5 of 8 gpt-oss). This supports running the planned replication with new seeds.
