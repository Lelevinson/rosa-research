## 2. Audit of every changed answer (measured, from fixture truth and the trace; AUDIT.md)
For each of the 8 changed answers the audit script re-derived from the fixture graph (not from the rewriter's ledger): the difference between the original and the submitted answer; that each new name equals the required identity of the task truth; that each short form was a **unique** whole-segment suffix among the graph names of its kind; that each full name is in the episode's ledger (see [LEDGER_DEFINITION.md](LEDGER_DEFINITION.md): as a contiguous string in a successful tool result in **1** episode (3 names), and in **7** episodes (12 names) only as the join of the `Node name` and `Node namespace` fields of one endpoint block of a successful topic-info result, which no tool output contains as one string); and that the strict scorer's issue list and missing-support list are empty after the rewrite. All 8 pass. I also read all 8 by hand: 4 are single `node` fields (`yeoman`, `bobbin` x3 -> the full name), 3 are three-key `gains` objects whose keys were bare (`mortar`, `xylem`, `zenith`) or partial (`nf/agate/xylem`, without the leading slash) and became the three full names with their values untouched, 1 is a two-entry `upstream` whose values were bare node names. No value was changed, dropped or moved, only names. **Independence note:** my audit is script + reading against the fixture truth, and its evidence check mirrors the ledger's join, so it is not independent of the ledger. Codex's independent review (`CODEX_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_20261117.md`) read the traces directly, agrees that all 8 changed answers equal the fixture answer with no other change, and corrected the evidence wording above (my first wording, "appears in a successful tool result", was too strong).
Every strict improvement is already lenient-supported in the original (lenient original 62 = strict applied 62): by the design's own reading these are **formatting/identity-presentation benefits**, not improvements on both scorers (no answer-level change). The design says a short answer identifies the right entity but does not meet the full-name contract of the task; the strict score is the one that asks for that contract.

## 3. Bars (frozen in PROTOCOL_NOTE.md, verbatim from the design) and reading
- **Safety FAIL** (a strict-supported answer that becomes unsupported, a wrong-identity rewrite, or an overwrite): **not triggered** (0, 0, 0; and 0 values lost: rule v2 cannot overwrite).
- **Eligible for consideration** needs zero harms among the actually rewritten answers (0 of 8), at least 8 episodes with an actual rewrite across at least 2 models and 2 templates (**8 episodes, 4 models, 2 templates**) and at least 2 strict unsupported -> supported episodes with verified identity and complete evidence (**8**). **All met.** This is "eligible for consideration", not "safe" or "recommended": the design says two improvements are repeated benefit, not power.
- **Where the evidence is thin (interpretation):** (1) the safety question that matters most for a feature that edits a submitted answer, whether it ever damages a correct answer, was exercised only through unchanged answers (54 supported exact answers untouched) and through unit tests; no correct short-form answer and no correct answer containing a name the rule could mis-resolve was rewritten. (2) the live traces never produced an ambiguous short name, an unseen full name or a key collision, although the fixture contains look-alikes (`nf_amb_*`) and an absent look-alike (`nf_absent`): the models gave full names there, so the branches that protect against wrong-identity rewrites are untested live. (3) all rewrites occurred in 4 of the 8 tasks and 4 models, with bonsai accounting for 4 of 8 (its short names are frequent).
- **The absent-name control (measured):** 7 of 8 supported; in all 7 the answer was the name exactly as given in the task, which the rule leaves unchanged because it is an exact ledger name (it is in the task text). The known suffix-repair harm mode needs an answer that is not an exact ledger name; none occurred.

## 4. Limits and next step
Designed stress cases, not a sample; one fixture family; two seeds; one pass of fixture-truth audit by the author. Nothing about held-out tasks or other prompts. A stronger test of the missing safety denominator would need cases where a model gives a wrong or look-alike short name, or a correct short name that the rule could resolve to a different entity, with enough episodes to rewrite originally supported answers; I did not run it (not authorized).
