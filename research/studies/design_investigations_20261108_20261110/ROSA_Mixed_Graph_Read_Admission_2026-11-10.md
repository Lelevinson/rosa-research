## Plans, actual interfaces and costs

No request above qualifies for a numerical comparison: **exact mixed, all-node and all-topic directional, per-component, greedy mixed, and full-graph snapshot costs are all not determined**. There are no justified pair sets, per-read costs, required setup or verified snapshots. The direct one-topic read in request 4 is relevant to the stated endpoint question, but its actual cost and completeness are unmeasured. A zero or tie for the six requests would incorrectly imply a defined optimization instance.

Conditional mathematical comparison: given a fixed, explicitly supplied set of required pairs, a valid node read and topic read each certifying the same membership facts, and known additive nonnegative entity costs, the exact mixed plan is a minimum-weight vertex cover of the bipartite obligation graph. All-node and all-topic reads are feasible covers; choosing the cheaper direction separately for each disconnected component is a feasible mixed cover; greedy mixed selection is another heuristic cover. The exact cover cannot cost more *within that additive model*, but may tie each alternative. A complete valid full-graph snapshot must also be compared as an ordinary alternative if it is actually available, with its collection and setup cost. It need not obey the additive vertex-cost model. No claim is made that any comparator wins end-to-end here.

The closest existing algorithm already solves the conditional exact selection problem: minimum-weight bipartite vertex cover is reducible to minimum cut and solvable in polynomial time ([Chlebík and Chlebíková, remark in §2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2004/101/download/)). Applying that method does not establish a new optimizer or prove that its assumptions hold for ROSA. The remaining possible contribution would require a separately demonstrated, general and fair way to obtain valid obligations and comparable evidence at lower total cost, not a renamed minimum-cut solution.

**Observed source, revision `94af17866cb82f3ea3f09422ef5cbb99b14cc89c`:** [`ros2_node_info` and `ros2_topic_info`](../../../src/rosa/tools/ros2.py#L174) accept lists but loop over entities and run a separate CLI subprocess for each; wrapper-call count is not underlying work. Node and topic lists are separate discovery commands. The installed Jazzy `ros2 node info` first checks node names, queries several categories, and hides hidden entities by default; `ros2 topic info --verbose` checks topic names and queries publisher/subscriber endpoint data and counts. They are not declared interchangeable contracts. Duplicate node names produce a warning; incomplete discovery, hidden topics, name/namespace ambiguity, output parsing, failures, changing graph state, and unsupported endpoints can invalidate a proposed equivalence or negative membership conclusion. Treat errors and unknowns as unknown, not absence. A publisher endpoint does not prove message delivery or a particular payload's authorship.

The inspected ROSA node/topic list and info wrappers do not provide a single documented atomic, complete full-graph snapshot. Building one through available enumeration and reads requires discovery, per-entity graph queries, parsing and freshness checks; its total cost and consistency are unknown. Installed `rclpy` exposes lower-level graph queries, but availability of those APIs does not establish an already registered ROSA snapshot tool, zero setup, atomicity, or a complete global view. A snapshot is a valid control only with equal visibility, freshness, identity and answer obligations; otherwise report it unavailable or unresolved. The default [ROSA prompt](../../../src/rosa/prompts.py#L81) forbids parallel/batched tool calls; a fair strong control must replace that restriction and actually use available batching/concurrency, while accounting for subprocesses, discovery, model turns/tokens, elapsed time, setup, output processing and retries. No latency or monetary costs were measured here.

## Decision record and boundary

- **Observed need:** 0 qualifying requests out of 6 inspected; no estimate of real workload frequency or avoidable native work. Discovery and wrong-source problems are alternative explanations, not evidence of mixed-selection savings.
- **Action contrast:** only conditional on independently grounded pair obligations, trustworthy common read semantics, observed costs and a valid freshness criterion; no qualifying real trace here shows a different action or net gain against the strongest control.
- **Prior mechanisms:** exact weighted bipartite cover/min-cut and ordinary directional, component, greedy and snapshot controls above. CPAW's [negative report](../cpaw_prototype_20261110/REPORT.md) concerns a different certificate/batching problem and reports its own strict default-registry eligibility, not a measurement of this workload.
- **Fair evaluation, if independently justified later:** preserve the original request and obtain obligations without a private semantic map; compare complete supported answers first with the same discovery, access, freshness, batching and retry rights, then include every setup, native read, token, decision and latency cost. The result of this check does not authorize such an experiment.
- **Stop rule applied:** stop at six requests or a first independently grounded positive case; six were reached without one. A request with explicit multiple pairs, validated compatible sources and measured total costs could change the workload finding; the missing focused document must also be inspected before claiming its protocol passed.

Only source reading and this report were produced. No optimizer was implemented, no LLM or ROS experiment was invoked, and no branch, commit, or push was made.
